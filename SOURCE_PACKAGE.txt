NDS4MiSTer public beta v0.3.0-beta.7 source - 2026-09-04
====================================================================

Source checkpoint: the LG-safe video path, current ARM-assisted 3D performance
work, expanded save-profile/Flash implementation, save-profile CDC fix,
controller touch, PSX-derived FPGA space efficiencies, DreamSTer-derived
full-frame renderer-fence batching, adaptive raster balancing, bounded
obsolete-geometry discard, cache-separated SPSC/futex synchronization, direct
completed-plane publication, and shadow-safe four-band raster work. It also
includes generation-safe empty-plane suppression and stabilized catch-up
pacing, fixes first-load save-sector alignment, preserves verified save mount
state across reset and direct-load transitions, and adds controller/mouse touch
input with a visible pointer. Beta.6 adds the accepted perspective-coordinate,
NEON vertex-transform, COLOR/NORMAL replay, and exact FPGA blend-shift work.
Beta.7 adds the compact writable boot-firmware subset, ARM7 Wi-Fi boot memory,
and cartridge-IR AUXSPI compatibility work contributed by InsaneFriend
(GitHub: saneFriend).

This curated source snapshot corresponds to the installable
NDS4MiSTer_Public_Beta_v0.3.0-beta.7_20260904 package. It retains the
LG-compatible video path and includes the FPGA-side MiSTer save bridge,
profile ROM, regular EEPROM/FRAM and Flash support, controller touch, and the
current hybrid 3D path. Exact artifact identities are listed below.

The release sound path is the GPL-licensed Nitro_DarkSide `nds_sound.vhd`
implementation built with `SOUND_ENABLE=1`. Retired private FPGA-sound
experiments are intentionally excluded and are not release dependencies.

Incremental beta.7 compatibility update
-----------------------------------------

* A compact 2 KiB writable firmware store implements the header, Wi-Fi, and
  user-settings pages used during boot, including SPI page-program writes.
  This is a source-defined behavioral model, not a console firmware dump.
* The ARM7 Wi-Fi aperture now provides its 8 KiB RAM and the boot-time
  register/baseband subset required by additional games. It does not implement
  wireless multiplayer or network connectivity.
* I-prefixed cartridges receive their retail IR transceiver AUXSPI command
  prefix before save-device traffic. Non-IR cartridge save behavior is
  unchanged.
* Hardware testing successfully ran Pokemon Platinum, Pokemon SoulSilver
  (including Continue without the earlier communication error), the full
  Mario Kart DS release, and the New Super Mario Bros. World 2-6 transition.
* The included 6.02 dB output attenuation only makes the existing audio
  distortion quieter. It is experimental and is not claimed as a sound fix.

Incremental beta.6 3D and FPGA update
--------------------------------------

* Perspective texture coordinates are evaluated in exact four-pixel batches,
  and the dominant GX vertex-transform block uses a bit-exact Cortex-A9 NEON
  path. More than one million randomized host and ARM checks matched the scalar
  reference.
* COLOR and NORMAL commands bypass unnecessary general replay setup while
  preserving the normal matrix, lighting, and fixed-point behavior.
* Focused measurements found roughly 3.6% lower GX processing cost and 5.6%
  lower geometry-flush cost across the retained ARM changes. The isolated
  vertex-transform block improved by 18.8%; that figure is not a whole-game or
  whole-renderer FPS claim. A conservative summary is about 5% lower ARM 3D
  processing cost, with the visible gain remaining scene-dependent.
* The FPGA blend path now expresses its exact divide-by-16 and divide-by-32
  operations as fixed shifts. The accepted fit saves 87 ALMs and 26 registers
  without changing DSP or RAM use.
* The FPS overlay now advances only when the FPGA accepts a changed 3D plane,
  instead of counting identical plane republications as fresh 3D motion.

Direct-plane and raster specialization update
---------------------------------------------

* Completed immutable 3D planes are published directly instead of being copied
  through an additional full-frame staging buffer.
* GX replay uses contiguous batches and specializations for the dominant packed
  vertex, cached texture, depth, opaque, and shadow paths. Bounded raster spans
  avoid software integer division while preserving exact fixed-point results.
* The four-band renderer accepts shadow work only after its ordering dependency
  is complete, retaining the faster parallel path without publishing partially
  ordered shadow results.
* A clean 25-second NSMB sample held the DS/game stream at 60.52 frames/s and
  published completed 3D descriptors at 60.48 frames/s with queue growth of
  only two packets, zero acknowledgement lag, and zero FPGA/HPS faults.
  Content sampling found about 34 visibly distinct 3D changes/s in that scene,
  confirming why the beta.5 on-screen counter could overstate unique motion
  when an identical plane was republished. Beta.6 corrects that counter.

ARM 3D frame-fence optimization
-------------------------------

* The plane-only MiSTer H3D renderer retains its complete-frame ownership
  fence while suppressing 192 unused scanline semaphore publications per
  changed frame.
* At the previously measured 57.3 published 3D FPS, this removes about 11,002
  mutex/condition-variable publications per second from the ARM render thread.
* The optimization does not skip frames or change pixels, polygon processing,
  frame ordering, saves, touch, sound, HDMI timing, or FPGA logic.
* Hardware acceptance found a large improvement in the New Super Mario Bros.
  final castle. A passive 60-second sample reached approximately 60 published
  3D FPS in lighter sections and 35-43 FPS in heavier sections, averaging
  49.6 FPS with no ARM/FPGA faults or sustained transport backlog.

ARM 3D adaptive-raster and catch-up optimization
------------------------------------------------

* Feedback-guided raster splitting balances polygon work across both Cortex-A9
  cores while retaining exact fixed-point raster results.
* Replay that misses its display deadline can discard obsolete geometry work
  instead of spending ARM time rendering a frame that can no longer be shown.
* Discard state remains attached to the incomplete polygon build buffer until
  the command stream reaches its real GX flush. This prevents the black areas,
  missing scenery, and stray polygons observed in the first Mario Kart test.
* A 32.4-second Mario Kart DS kiosk-demo sample measured 59.8 input frames/s,
  56.3 rendered 3D frames/s, and 55.9 published 3D frames/s. The replay backlog
  recovered from 6 frames to 0, with no FPGA or ARM/HPS faults.

DreamSTer-style ARM synchronization
-----------------------------------

* Cache-separated SPSC producer/consumer indices replace the mutex on the
  fixed replay arena. Private Linux futexes are used only when the queue is
  truly empty or full.
* The headless renderer uses the same atomic-counting/futex approach. No
  packet, command, frame, polygon, or raster result is skipped.
* Focused A/B measurements found 4.8% lower end-to-end packet retirement time,
  8.0% lower renderer handoff latency, and 29.9% lower burst-handoff time.
* A demanding live sample rendered and published 532 3D frames in 9.569
  seconds: 55.6 FPS, or 92.7% of the 60 FPS target, with no ARM/HPS or FPGA
  faults. Scene-to-scene variation prevents treating this as a controlled
  whole-game FPS percentage against the previous beta.

Catch-up visibility and frame pacing
------------------------------------

* Catch-up discards carry a monotonically increasing visibility generation.
  An older asynchronous render therefore cannot clear a newer discard state
  or publish its derived empty plane over the last valid 3D result.
* Mild catch-up preserves the complete GX geometry build and skips only the
  expensive raster pass. More destructive third/quarter/eighth-rate tiers are
  reserved for measured replay leads of 8/12/16 frames instead of oscillating
  at the normal four-to-six-frame heavy-scene range.
* In the accepted NSMB castle sample, input and replay both held 59.8 frames/s,
  raster publication held 33.4 frames/s under sustained load, replay backlog
  remained bounded at 2-6 frames, and FPGA/HPS fault counts stayed zero. The
  maintainer reported substantially smoother movement with only minor residual
  jerkiness.

FPGA space efficiencies
-----------------------

* Shared ARM7 barrel-shifter datapath with unchanged instruction latency
* Exact two-level cartridge-save profile lookup
* Packed per-channel FPGA sound fetch state
* Packed ARM9 instruction/data cache-tag storage
* The attempted IPC MLAB mapping was rejected from the candidate after real
  Quartus data showed that it expanded into logic; the known-good IPC RAM
  implementation is retained.

Save support
------------

* MiSTer standard save interface and /media/fat/saves/NDS location
* 512-byte tiny EEPROM plus 8, 64, and 128 KiB regular EEPROM saves
* 256 KiB, 512 KiB, and 1 MiB Flash saves
* Save-device profiles generated from the vendored melonDS ROM database
* Existing-save loading, dirty flushing, and persistence across ROM reloads
* Safe handling of read-only and invalid-size images
* Cartridge saves are supported; emulator save states are not
* First-load sector data is indexed from the private transfer word count, not
  the shared MiSTer buffer address, preventing the measured 19-word offset.
* Verified cartridge/save epochs survive reset and direct-load transitions.

Reset was verified with New Super Mario Bros. after preserving the cartridge
and save mount. Reselecting the ROM remains the fallback for titles that do
not reset cleanly.

Older experimental builds may already have created an incorrectly sized or
corrupted `.sav` file. If a game still reports save corruption, back up and
then delete or move that game's existing file from `/media/fat/saves/NDS/` so
the game can create a fresh save. This release does not attempt to repair
corrupted legacy data.

Touch support
-------------

* Move the controller's right stick to select an absolute DS touchscreen
  position. Centered is approximately the center of the 256x192 touch canvas.
* Hold the remappable `Touch` action to press at that position, and release it
  to lift the stylus. MiSTer's controller-remapping menu can assign `Touch` to
  a preferred button.
* The SPI touchscreen returns melonDS-compatible 12-bit coordinate samples.
* Direct boot installs matching touchscreen calibration data.
* Relative MiSTer mouse motion controls the stylus, and the left mouse button
  presses it. The most recently moved mouse or right stick owns the pointer.
* The pointer is white while hovering, red while pressed, stays visible during
  a press, and lingers for about half a second after movement.
* Engine B is not displayed yet, so precise interaction with touchscreen
  graphics remains limited.

Verification
------------

The focused regression passed save-profile lookup and bundled CDC publication,
all supported EEPROM/Flash sizes, EEPROM and Flash command behavior, dirty
flushes, read-only and invalid-size behavior, replacement-ROM mount ordering,
cross-size isolation, right-stick touch coordinates, pen release, SPI touch,
direct-boot calibration, writable firmware SPI behavior, all 4096 Wi-Fi RAM
halfwords through the tested driver patterns, cartridge-IR AUXSPI behavior,
and the complete Nitro console-island host RTL gate.

Quartus Prime 17.0.2 completed map, fit, assembly, and timing analysis. The
successful Seed-2 fit used 41,281 of 41,910 ALMs (98%), 45,467 registers,
3,508,257 of 5,662,720 block-memory bits (62%), 484 of 553 RAM blocks (88%),
69 DSP blocks, and four PLLs. Assembly completed with device checksum
0x10313EB0. Static timing remains diagnostic information rather than a
deployment gate for this experimental project. This exact RBF was direct-
loaded with FPGA state `operating`, core identity `NDS`, and exactly one
verified ARM service at 1 GHz. The maintainer approved the compatibility tests
listed in the beta.7 update above after verifying the exact release hashes.

Package exclusions
------------------
